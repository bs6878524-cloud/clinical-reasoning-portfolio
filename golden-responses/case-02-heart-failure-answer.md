# Case 02: Heart Failure Exacerbation — Golden Response

## Problem Representation
Older adult with known heart failure presenting with dyspnea, orthopnea, hypoxia, bilateral edema, elevated JVP, crackles, and pulmonary congestion on chest x-ray, consistent with acute decompensated heart failure.

## Assessment
This presentation is most consistent with acute volume overload from heart failure exacerbation. The patient is currently hypertensive and hypoxic but not in shock. Immediate priorities are decongestion, oxygen support, and evaluation for the precipitating cause.

## Differential Diagnosis
Important alternatives include pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, acute coronary syndrome, and COPD exacerbation. However, the combination of orthopnea, edema, elevated JVP, crackles, and imaging evidence of congestion strongly supports heart failure exacerbation.

## Plan
1. Start loop diuretic therapy promptly.
2. Provide supplemental oxygen as needed.
3. Monitor urine output, weight, electrolytes, and renal function.
4. Restrict sodium and fluid intake.
5. Review adherence to heart failure medications and recent diet or medication changes.
6. Evaluate for triggers such as arrhythmia, ischemia, renal dysfunction, or infection.
7. Escalate care if respiratory status worsens or if diuresis is inadequate.

## Clinical Priorities
- Decongest the patient.
- Identify the trigger.
- Protect kidney function and electrolytes.
- Monitor for respiratory decline.

## Key Safety Points
- Diuresis should be monitored closely.
- Renal function and potassium can worsen with treatment.
- Hypoxia requires prompt support.
- A precipitating cause should always be sought.
