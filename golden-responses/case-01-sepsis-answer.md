# Case 01: Sepsis — Golden Response

## Problem Representation
Older adult with fever, hypotension, tachycardia, altered mental status, elevated lactate, and urinalysis suggestive of infection, concerning for sepsis with likely urinary source.

## Assessment
This patient has sepsis with concern for septic shock given hypotension and elevated lactate. Immediate management should prioritize rapid resuscitation, early antibiotics, and source identification.

## Differential Diagnosis
The most likely diagnosis is urosepsis, but pneumonia, intra-abdominal infection, and other occult sources should also be considered. Noninfectious causes of hypotension such as dehydration or medication effect may coexist but do not explain the full syndrome.

## Plan
1. Activate sepsis protocol immediately.
2. Obtain blood cultures and urine cultures before antibiotics if this does not delay treatment.
3. Start broad-spectrum IV antibiotics with urinary source coverage, adjusted for renal function.
4. Give aggressive IV fluids and reassess blood pressure, mental status, and urine output.
5. Check lactate again after resuscitation.
6. Monitor for vasopressor need if hypotension persists after fluids.
7. Search for and treat the source of infection.
8. Review home medications and kidney function to guide dosing and avoid nephrotoxicity.

## Clinical Priorities
- Treat as septic shock until proven otherwise.
- Do not delay antibiotics.
- Monitor hemodynamics closely.
- Adjust therapy to culture results and clinical response.

## Key Safety Points
- Early treatment is essential.
- Renal dysfunction should affect antibiotic choice and dosing.
- Persistent hypotension requires escalation of care.
- Altered mental status may reflect poor perfusion and infection severity.
