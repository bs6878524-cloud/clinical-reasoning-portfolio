# Case 03: Delirium — Golden Response

## Problem Representation
Older adult with acute fluctuating confusion, inattention, agitation, and disorientation, consistent with delirium, likely multifactorial.

## Assessment
This is delirium until proven otherwise. The most important next step is to identify and correct the underlying cause or causes, while maintaining patient safety and minimizing factors that worsen confusion.

## Differential Diagnosis
Possible causes include infection, medication toxicity or withdrawal, dehydration, metabolic derangements, urinary retention, constipation, hypoxia, and less commonly stroke or other neurologic disease. Primary psychiatric illness is less likely given the acute fluctuating course.

## Plan
1. Evaluate for reversible causes, including infection, metabolic abnormalities, medication effects, urinary retention, constipation, and dehydration.
2. Review the medication list carefully for high-risk or deliriogenic drugs.
3. Provide hydration and correct metabolic abnormalities as indicated.
4. Use frequent reorientation and a calm environment.
5. Ensure safety with close observation and fall precautions.
6. Avoid unnecessary sedatives and restraints when possible.
7. Escalate neurologic workup if focal findings or other red flags appear.

## Clinical Priorities
- Identify the cause.
- Protect the patient from injury.
- Reduce contributors to delirium.
- Use nonpharmacologic measures first whenever possible.

## Key Safety Points
- Delirium is a medical emergency, not a psychiatric diagnosis by default.
- Medication review is essential.
- A fluctuating course strongly supports delirium.
- New focal neurologic signs require further evaluation.
