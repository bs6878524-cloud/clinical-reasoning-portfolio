# Case 02: Heart Failure Exacerbation

## Chief Complaint
Shortness of breath and leg swelling.

## History of Present Illness
A 72-year-old patient with known heart failure presents with 3 days of worsening dyspnea, orthopnea, and bilateral leg swelling. The patient reports weight gain and difficulty lying flat. On exam, blood pressure is 148/86 mmHg, heart rate is 104 bpm, respiratory rate is 22/min, oxygen saturation is 90% on room air, and there are bibasilar crackles and pitting edema.

## Relevant History
- Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction
- Hypertension
- Chronic kidney disease

## Exam
- Mild respiratory distress
- Elevated jugular venous pressure
- Bibasilar crackles
- Bilateral lower extremity edema

## Labs and Studies
- BNP elevated
- Chest x-ray with pulmonary congestion
- Creatinine mildly above baseline
- ECG without acute ischemic changes

## Problem Representation
Older adult with known heart failure, dyspnea, orthopnea, edema, hypoxia, and evidence of volume overload, consistent with acute decompensated heart failure.

## Differential Diagnosis
- Acute decompensated heart failure
- Pneumonia
- Pulmonary embolism
- Acute coronary syndrome
- COPD exacerbation

## Assessment
This is likely volume overload from heart failure exacerbation, and the immediate priority is diuresis, oxygen support, and evaluation for triggers such as medication nonadherence, dietary indiscretion, arrhythmia, ischemia, or renal dysfunction.

## Plan
- Give loop diuretic therapy
- Monitor oxygenation and urine output
- Restrict sodium and fluid intake
- Check electrolytes and renal function
- Evaluate for precipitating factors
- Escalate care if respiratory status worsens
