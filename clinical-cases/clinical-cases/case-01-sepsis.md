# Case 01: Sepsis

## Chief Complaint
Fever, confusion, and low blood pressure.

## History of Present Illness
A 68-year-old patient presents with 2 days of fever, chills, weakness, and worsening confusion. Family reports decreased oral intake and reduced urine output. In the emergency department, blood pressure is 88/54 mmHg, heart rate is 118 bpm, respiratory rate is 24/min, temperature is 39.1 C, and oxygen saturation is 94% on room air.

## Relevant History
- Hypertension
- Type 2 diabetes mellitus
- Chronic kidney disease stage 3

## Exam
- Ill-appearing
- Dry mucous membranes
- Confused but arousable
- No focal neurologic deficits

## Labs and Studies
- WBC elevated
- Lactate elevated
- Creatinine above baseline
- Urinalysis suggestive of infection

## Problem Representation
Older adult with fever, hypotension, tachycardia, altered mental status, and evidence of infection, concerning for sepsis with possible urinary source.

## Differential Diagnosis
- Sepsis from urinary tract infection
- Pneumonia
- Intra-abdominal infection
- Medication-related hypotension
- Dehydration with superimposed infection

## Assessment
This patient has septic shock until proven otherwise and requires immediate resuscitation, cultures, broad-spectrum antibiotics, and source evaluation.

## Plan
- Activate sepsis protocol
- Obtain blood and urine cultures
- Start broad-spectrum IV antibiotics
- Give IV fluids
- Monitor lactate and urine output
- Assess need for vasopressors if hypotension persists
- Identify and treat source of infection
