# Case 03: Delirium

## Chief Complaint
Acute confusion and agitation.

## History of Present Illness
An 81-year-old patient is brought in for 1 day of worsening confusion, agitation, and poor sleep. Family says the patient was previously alert and oriented at baseline but became disoriented overnight. There is no clear focal weakness or speech deficit. Vital signs are stable, but the patient is inattentive and intermittently trying to get out of bed.

## Relevant History
- Hypertension
- Chronic kidney disease
- Recent hospitalization
- Polypharmacy

## Exam
- Fluctuating attention
- Disoriented to time and place
- No focal neurologic deficits
- Dry mucous membranes

## Labs and Studies
- Basic labs show mild dehydration
- UA may be equivocal
- No clear structural abnormality on initial evaluation

## Problem Representation
Older adult with acute fluctuating mental status, inattention, and agitation, consistent with delirium until proven otherwise.

## Differential Diagnosis
- Delirium from infection
- Medication effect or withdrawal
- Dehydration or metabolic abnormality
- Urinary retention or constipation
- Stroke or other neurologic cause
- Primary psychiatric disease

## Assessment
This presentation is most consistent with delirium, likely multifactorial, and requires prompt identification of reversible causes, medication review, hydration, and safety measures.

## Plan
- Assess for infection, metabolic derangements, and medication triggers
- Review recent medication changes
- Provide hydration and correct reversible causes
- Reorient frequently
- Use delirium precautions
- Avoid unnecessary restraints and sedatives when possible
- Escalate evaluation if focal neurologic signs or other red flags appear
